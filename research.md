My research is multidisciplinary, with a strong focus on biomedical applications.
I tackled challenges in different modalities such as computer vision, tabular data, and token sequences of clinical data, specifically when they are **scarce, high-dimensional or noisy**. My main tools are deep and statistical learning.

<section class="topic" markdown="1">
## Characterization of clinical trajectories <span class="topic-when">2025 – now</span>

At [CBIO](https://cbio.mines-paristech.fr/) (Mines Paris – PSL & Institut Curie), I work with Eloïse Berson on **longitudinal electronic health records (EHR)**.
I build **deep sequential models** (GRU, transformer, neural ODEs) that predict **patient survival** from sparse, irregular follow-up data, and study how foundation models can help design better patient cohorts for biomedical discovery. The main focus of the project is to then be able to **characterize patients trajectories** for long-term illnesses such as cognitive decline (e.g., Alzheimer's disease), with also a parallel interest in mid-term cancer such as breast, lung, pancreatic and brain cancers. Since 2026 this work is funded by a PR[AI]RIE AI Fellowship.
</section>

<section class="topic" markdown="1">
## Accelerating numerical simulations with AI <span class="topic-when">2024</span>

While the start-up NPCo (ex-Augur) was being developed at Inria Start-Up Studio incubator, with [Emmanuel Menier](https://emenier.com/) and [Matthieu Nastorg](https://www.linkedin.com/in/mnastorg/), I benchmarked deep learning surrogates for **numerical simulations on 2D and 3D meshes**.
These models predict physical fields (e.g., temperature, pressure) and physical scalar values (e.g., drag coefficients) much faster than classical solvers, to speed up the design of wind turbines, rotors or medical devices for example.
</section>

<section class="topic" markdown="1">
## Generative models for transcriptomics <span class="topic-when">PhD, 2021 – 2024</span>

Deep learning requires many training samples, yet gene expression cohorts are small (a few hundred patients for ~20,000 genes in bulk-RNA data), leading to a small *n*, large *p* problem.
During my PhD with [Blaise Hanczar](https://sites.google.com/site/bhanczarhomepage/) (IBISC) and [Michèle Sebag](https://scholar.google.fr/citations?user=TNMDbO8AAAAJ&hl=fr) (Inria TAU), I used **deep generative models for data augmentation** to improve cancer phenotype prediction from RNA-seq and microarray data. I benchmarked **VAEs, GANs and diffusion models** for transcriptomics, with adapted quality metrics ([Bioinformatics 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btad239), [BMC Bioinformatics 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12859-026-06470-8)). I also proposed **GMDA**, a frugal, non-adversarial generative model for tabular data ([ECML-PKDD 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-70371-3_4)).
</section>

<section class="topic" markdown="1">
## Covid-19 forecasting <span class="topic-when">2020 – 2021</span>

As a finalist of the **XPRIZE Pandemic Response Challenge**, I worked on LSTM models to forecast infection rates across +40 countries.
I then worked as an intern with [Isabelle Guyon](https://guyon.chalearn.org/) at Inria to combine **epidemiological compartmental models** (SIR) with **machine-learning confidence intervals** (ML-CI) built from random forests.
</section>

## Deep learning for astrophysics <span class="topic-when">2019</span>

My first research internship experience, with [Samuel Farrens](https://sfarrens.github.io/) at Cosmostat (CEA), was about using **transfer learning** (a fine-tuned VGG-16) to detect blended galaxies in simulated survey images ([A&A 2022](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202141166)).
